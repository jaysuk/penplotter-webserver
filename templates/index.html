      Timelapse

      Takes a picture of the plotter every few seconds while it plots and makes a video from them (video needs ffmpeg). The camera is a picture address (for example a webcam server's snapshot link), a Raspberry Pi camera (rpicam-still) or a USB webcam (fswebcam).

      Pi Plot shield buttons

      The two buttons on the shield (GPIO 27 and 22). Needs the gpiozero package; on a Raspberry Pi 5 also rpi-lgpio. Leave this off without the shield.

      Login

      Requires a user name and password (HTTP basic auth) to use the web page. Clear the user name to turn the login off. A change applies immediately, so your browser will ask for the login after saving.

      The login travels in clear text over plain HTTP, so it only keeps out people who cannot watch your network. For HTTPS give a certificate and its key (full paths, for example made with the openssl command in the README). Wrong passwords are counted: an address that sends ten within five minutes is refused for five minutes. The certificate is read when the server starts, so restart the service after changing it.

      Your plotters

      The plotters you saved are kept in userdata/plotters.json, which an update leaves alone, and are part of the backup below. Export them to a file to share or to keep, or import a file somebody sent.

      Backup

      A zip with the settings, your plotters, the plot history, the presets and the queue. It includes the passwords in config.ini, so keep it private.

      vpype devices

      vpype turns a drawing into HPGL for a device: the size of one plotter unit, the pens, and for each paper size where the plotter starts counting. A plotter that is not in the Plotting Device list needs a device of its own. They are kept in userdata/vpype_devices.toml, which an update leaves alone, and are part of the backup.

        Resume this plot?

        The plot carries on from where it got to. Only continue if neither the paper nor the pen carriage has moved since it stopped, and the same pen is loaded.

        Plotters without buffer feedback may have had a little left to draw when the plot stopped. Going back a little draws the last part again instead of leaving a gap.

        This plot was cut short by a power cut or a restart. The place it carries on from was noted about every 30 seconds, and the plotter may have moved the paper or the pen carriage, or forgotten where its origin is: check them and the loaded pen first. The plot may be drawn again from a little before where it stopped.
